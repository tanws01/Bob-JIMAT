# 1. The Problem

## Everyone overpays for groceries — and most people don't know it.

Buying groceries sounds simple:

> Make a list → Go to a store → Buy everything.

But the reality is more complicated.

### 🧾 Prices are scattered

The same grocery item can have different prices at different stores, but shoppers rarely have an easy way to compare their **entire basket**.

### 🚗 Cheap doesn't always mean cheaper

The store with the cheapest products may be further away.

A RM5 saving can disappear once you consider petrol, travel distance and time.

### 🛒 People optimise one store, not the whole basket

Most shoppers choose one familiar supermarket and buy everything there.

They don't know whether splitting a basket across two nearby stores could save them significantly more.

### 🤔 The best shopping strategy is difficult to calculate

To make the smartest decision, a shopper would need to consider:

**Items + Prices + Stores + Distance + Number of Stops + Shopping Effort**

Doing this manually is time-consuming and unrealistic for an ordinary grocery trip.

### 💸 Savings disappear without being tracked

Even when shoppers find a cheaper option, they rarely know:

* How much they actually saved
* How much that becomes over a year
* Whether their grocery spending is improving
* How today's prices compare with recent prices

**The problem isn't simply that groceries are expensive.**

The problem is that shoppers don't have a simple way to turn fragmented price information into a smarter shopping decision.

# 4. The Solution

## One grocery list in. Three ready-to-shop plans out.

JIMAT takes a shopper's grocery list and automatically analyses nearby stores, real prices and travel distance.

Instead of giving users a giant price comparison table, JIMAT gives them **three practical strategies**.

### 🟢 Best Value

**The smartest balance between savings and effort.**

JIMAT looks for the best overall value, usually using a tight two-store split where the additional savings justify the additional travel.

**For shoppers who want to save without making grocery shopping complicated.**

---

### 🔵 Most Convenient

**Everything in one trip.**

JIMAT finds the strongest single-store option while prioritising coverage and convenience.

**For shoppers who value time and simplicity.**

---

### 🟠 Cheapest Possible

**Maximum savings on the basket.**

JIMAT finds the lowest available price for each item, even if that means visiting more stores.

**For shoppers who are willing to spend more time travelling to minimise the basket cost.**

# 6. End-to-End User Flow

## Step 1 — Start

### Landing

The shopper enters JIMAT with one simple promise:

> **Shop Smarter. Save More.**

They start by creating their grocery plan.

---

## Step 2 — Set Location

The shopper tells JIMAT where they shop:

* State
* District
* Maximum travel distance

The search radius can be set up to **15 km**.

This gives JIMAT the geographic context needed to compare nearby stores.

---

## Step 3 — Tell JIMAT About Your Household

The shopper can provide:

* Household size
* Optional monthly grocery budget

This helps put the grocery basket into a household spending context.

---

## Step 4 — Build Your Grocery Basket

The shopper browses or searches across six real DOSM grocery categories:

1. Fresh Produce
2. Pantry Staples
3. Packaged & Cooking
4. Drinks & Dairy
5. Milk & Baby
6. Daily Necessities

They add:

**Item → Quantity**

Nothing is preselected.

The basket represents what the shopper actually needs.

---

## Step 5 — Review Your Basket

JIMAT calculates an initial estimate using average shelf prices.

This becomes the shopper's:

### Savings Baseline

It answers:

> **"What would this basket typically cost before optimisation?"**

This baseline is later used to calculate the shopper's savings.

---

## Step 6 — JIMAT Optimises

This is the main **"wow" moment**.

JIMAT scans available stores and prices, then calculates:

**Price × Quantity × Store × Distance × Number of Stops**

The shopper sees an animated optimisation experience while JIMAT crunches the basket.

Instead of manually comparing dozens of stores, the shopper waits a few seconds.

---

## Step 7 — Compare Three Strategies

JIMAT presents three understandable choices:

| Strategy             | What it optimises   | Typical approach      |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| 🟢 Best Value        | Savings + effort    | Tight two-store split |
| 🔵 Most Convenient   | Time + simplicity   | One-store trip        |
| 🟠 Cheapest Possible | Lowest basket price | Multiple stores       |

Each plan shows the information the shopper actually needs:

* Estimated total cost
* Estimated savings
* Number of stores
* Estimated travel distance
* Items per store
* Store names
* Store addresses

The shopper chooses the strategy that fits their priorities.

---

## Step 8 — Get Your Shopping Plan

Once a strategy is selected, JIMAT turns it into a practical checklist.

For every store, the shopper sees:

**Store → Address → Items → Quantity → Subtotal**

Real DOSM store addresses are shown.

Navigation is available through:

* Google Maps
* Waze

The optimisation is now converted into something the shopper can actually execute.

---

## Step 9 — Shopping Mode

While shopping, the user can tick off items.

A live progress bar shows:

**0% → 25% → 50% → 75% → 100%**

The goal is to keep the experience simple:

> **Follow the plan. Tick the items. Finish the shop.**

## Step 10 — Confirm Your Actual Spending

After shopping, the user enters what they actually spent.

JIMAT compares:

**Baseline Cost → Optimised Plan → Actual Spend**

The shopper can now see their realised savings.

What the savings could look like if similar savings happen repeatedly.

### 🥦 Grocery Health Score

A simple indicator of the overall grocery basket.

### 📈 Price Intelligence

Compare current prices against recent prices, including:

### 🌱 Five-Year Savings Journey

Show how recurring grocery savings can compound over time.

The idea is simple:

# 9. Real Malaysian Data

JIMAT is powered by real **DOSM PriceCatcher open data**.

The current snapshot contains:

* **301 real grocery items**
* **6 grocery categories**
* **2,140 real premises**
* **All 16 Malaysian states**
* **291 items priced in the current month**
* **10 items backfilled from the most recent available month**

Store names, addresses and prices come from real DOSM data.

JIMAT does not invent prices for products that DOSM does not track.

For example, if a product has no real DOSM price data, it is not artificially added simply to make the catalogue look bigger.

# 10. Data Freshness & Honesty

JIMAT uses a hybrid approach.

### Prices

PriceCatcher prices are stored as a dated snapshot in `snapshot.js`.

This allows JIMAT to:

* Work offline
* Run directly from `file://`
* Avoid requiring a backend
* Preserve a reproducible dataset

When an item is not priced in the current month, JIMAT can use its most recent available DOSM price from up to 12 months of historical data.

The interface clearly labels these backfilled prices with their relevant month.

### Inflation

DOSM CPI data can be fetched live and used as a freshness indicator.

If the live request is unavailable, JIMAT falls back to the baked snapshot.

### Distance

Distances are estimates based on geocoded district centroids.

They are not intended to replace real navigation.

For exact routing, JIMAT links users directly to:

**Google Maps / Waze**

# 14. Optimisation Logic

JIMAT calculates a baseline first:

```text
Baseline =
Σ (average shelf price × quantity)
```

It then identifies candidate stores within the user's selected travel radius.

If suitable stores are unavailable, JIMAT falls back to the nearest available options.

### Best Value

JIMAT starts with the strongest single-store option and considers adding one complementary store when the additional saving justifies the additional travel.

### Most Convenient

JIMAT selects a single store that provides strong product coverage while keeping the shopping trip simple.

### Cheapest Possible

JIMAT finds the lowest available price for each item, regardless of how many stores are required.

Each strategy considers travel through a return-trip travel penalty and evaluates the balance between:

**Savings + Coverage + Effort**
